Top 10 Learnings
**Finetuning LLMs requires strategic layer selection** — It’s not necessary to finetune all layers of a large language model (LLM) for effective classification; focusing on the last token can capture contextual information better. This insight has shifted my approach to model training, emphasizing efficiency and targeted adjustments. (Source: Building A GPT-Style LLM Classifier From Scratch — article, October)

**Adaptive learning is crucial for sales performance** — Employees with a so-called "sales gene" can learn and adapt quickly to new information, outperforming those with more experience but less adaptability. This challenges traditional hiring practices and suggests that organizations should prioritize adaptive learning capabilities over seniority. (Source: There Really Is a “Sales Gene” — article, October)

**AI use in organizations requires R&D investment** — Many companies struggle to leverage AI effectively because they rely on external consultants instead of developing their own R&D capabilities. I now see the need for organizations to invest in internal experimentation to harness AI’s full potential. (Source: AI in organizations: Some tactics — article, October)

**The importance of continuous improvement in teamwork** — Establishing a system for continuous improvement and innovation within teams is essential for adapting to new technologies and enhancing collaboration. This has prompted me to advocate for more structured feedback mechanisms in my team. (Source: New Rules for Teamwork — article, October)

**Cognitive biases affect AI evaluations** — The halo effect and processing fluency can skew perceptions of AI outputs, leading to misjudgments about their accuracy. This has made me more cautious in evaluating AI-generated content, emphasizing the need for critical assessment. (Source: How Amazon is Rethinking human evaluation for generative large language models — article, October)

**RAG architecture is gaining traction** — Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become a popular architecture for LLMs, combining the strengths of retrieval and generation. This trend suggests a shift toward more integrated AI systems that leverage external information effectively. (Source: Advanced RAG Techniques: an Illustrated Overview — article, October)

**AI-driven natural language interfaces democratize robotics** — The integration of AI with robotics allows non-technical users to interact with robots using everyday language, broadening the accessibility of robotic technologies. This realization has influenced my perspective on the future of human-robot interaction. (Source: Generative AI and Robotics in 2024 — article, October)

**The role of generative AI in creative tasks** — Generative AI models are increasingly being used to enhance creativity in various fields, from content creation to design. This has led me to explore how these tools can be integrated into my own creative processes. (Source: A New Way to Control Language Model Generations — article, October)

**Organizational dynamics can hinder leadership effectiveness** — Dysfunctional leadership teams, characterized by hyper-competition or complacency, can significantly impede organizational success. This insight has prompted me to reflect on the dynamics within my own leadership team and consider strategies for improvement. (Source: Why Leadership Teams Fail — article, October)

**AI models struggle with factual accuracy as they scale** — Larger language models may produce more complex responses but often at the cost of reliability. This has reinforced my understanding of the importance of critical thinking when engaging with AI outputs. (Source: Larger and more instructable language models become less reliable — article, October)
